# Write As Physicist

Make the physical question, evidence, and result easy to find and follow. Match each claim's strength and scope to the work. Apply these principles across experimental, theoretical, and computational physics. Choose examples suited to the task; use varied fields when explaining general principles.

## Work from the science

1. Identify the deliverable, audience, language, and length. Follow the supplied journal conventions and preserve the author's useful voice.
2. Establish the question, main result, and connecting evidence. Sketch this argument before drafting a substantial text; keep a local edit local.
3. Use the supplied data, equations, figures, and references as the factual basis. Preserve quantities, units, signs, uncertainties, assumptions, citations, and the distinction between measurement and interpretation.
4. Resolve wording from context. Mark missing facts that affect correctness, ask focused questions, and continue with the parts that can be completed.
5. Revise the argument first, then paragraphs, sentences, notation, and formatting.

## Build a readable argument

Order the paper by the dependencies of its argument: introduce observations and definitions before drawing on them. Include contradictory observations and informative null results where they affect the explanation.

Give each paragraph a clear job: usually state its point early, support it, and connect it to the next step. Order sentences so readers can follow without reconstructing missing steps. Use familiar information to introduce new information when it helps continuity.

Make physical objects or actions the subjects where useful: the correlation length grows, the Hall response changes, the calculation predicts, the fit yields. Choose active or passive voice according to the focus, and use consistent terms for each quantity or mechanism.

Connect paragraphs through their content. Add transitions for real relations, such as consequences, contrasts, conditions, or changes of scale. Let paragraph length and sentence rhythm follow the thought.

## Give each section a purpose

| Section | What the reader should learn |
| --- | --- |
| Title | The specific phenomenon, system, question, or supported finding. |
| Abstract | The problem, approach, main result, resulting understanding, and essential conditions. |
| Introduction | Established knowledge, the unresolved issue, its importance, and what this work asks or tests. |
| Methods | How evidence was produced and analyzed, including choices needed to assess or reproduce it. |
| Results | What was measured or calculated, relevant comparisons, and quantitative evidence. |
| Discussion | How evidence answers the question, how explanations compare, and which uncertainties affect inference. |
| Conclusion | The supported answer and its concrete implication or remaining question. |

Adapt the structure to the genre. Theory may proceed through a model, derivation, limiting cases, and predictions. Combined Results and Discussion can interpret each result in place. Organize reviews around questions, evidence, or disagreements, explaining how studies fit together.

Write or revise the abstract once the argument is stable; check every claim against the body. State the contribution specifically enough for readers to judge its value.

## Calibrate claims precisely

Distinguish observations, fits, calculations, inferences, and proposals. Use verbs that express those relationships: calculations predict within models; measurements resolve features within instrumental capabilities; interpretations connect evidence to explanations.

Put conditions beside the claims they govern. Identify consequential approximations, ranges, resolution, uncertainty, and competing explanations. Carry essential conditions into abstracts and captions that must stand alone.

Give qualifications identifiable work:

- **Range:** specify temperatures, fields, energies, times, or system sizes covered.
- **Model:** identify assumptions and approximations.
- **Measurement:** distinguish an unresolved feature from an absent one; base detection bounds on sensitivity analysis.
- **Inference:** state which interpretations the evidence supports or distinguishes.
- **Uncertainty:** define reported intervals, error estimates, or sensitivity where known.

Separate uncertainty in a measured quantity from uncertainty about its mechanism. Keep established observations direct while their explanation remains open. Preserve negative conclusions that establish exclusions, detection limits, or failed predictions.

## Remove defensive padding

Use this working definition: **over-defensive writing anticipates objections through qualifications, disclaimers, denials, or explanations that add no useful information about the claim or the reader's task.**

Identify the proposition a suspect sentence changes. What would readers understand differently without it? Retain information that affects their understanding of the evidence, scope, reasoning, or relevant action. Remove repeated assurances and imagined disputes that add none.

| Habit to revise | Editing action |
| --- | --- |
| Opening with what the text will not do | Start with the question, result, or action; state scope where needed. |
| Stacking hedges around one uncertainty | Express it once, precisely. Check whether qualifiers govern different parts of the claim. |
| Repeated “only,” “merely,” or “just” | Name the contribution and its extent. |
| Adding “X does not mean Y” after X is clear | Explain Y when it is a live issue in the argument or user's question. Otherwise remove it. |
| Routine “not X but Y” framing | State Y directly; keep contrasts whose distinction matters. |
| An exception or apology after every claim | Attach substantive conditions to their claims and consolidate repetition. |
| Calling the writing careful, rigorous, balanced, or responsible | Supply the evidence and reasoning for readers to judge. |
| Generic reminders or renewed disclaimers at the end | Finish with the supported answer, consequence, or specific unresolved question. |

Review Chinese forms such as “需要强调的是”, “严格来说”, “这并不意味着”, “X 不代表 Y”, “并不是……而是……”, and “当然，仍需……” by function. Keep useful qualifications and genuine contrasts expressed in these forms.

## Inspect the ending separately

Read the final paragraph separately once the draft is complete. End with a result, implication, concrete next question, or requested action. A null result or identifiability limit can be the main finding.

Delete endings that repeat an already clear boundary, warn against an unraised interpretation, announce caution, or say only “more research is needed.” For useful further work, name the unresolved question and the observation or calculation that would address it.

Once the ending has done its job, stop.

## Explain equations as part of the argument

Introduce what each equation expresses and why it is needed. Define symbols at first use, keep notation stable, and state units or dimensional conventions where relevant. Integrate equations grammatically and punctuate them.

Distinguish identities, definitions, approximations, phenomenological relations, and fitted expressions. State assumptions where they enter; explain physical meaning and limiting behavior, with enough intermediate reasoning for the intended reader.

Refer to equations by what they establish. Where clearer, replace “using Eq. (1) in Eq. (2)” with the actual mathematical or physical operation. Check notation, dimensions, signs, normalization, and limits against the supplied material.

## Write captions that make figures readable

Make the figure and caption a coherent unit: identify the system, map the panels, and explain what is plotted. Select the needed components:

1. **Subject or finding:** open with a short description or supported result, following the venue's convention.
2. **Panel map:** descriptions in displayed order, distinguishing measurements, calculations, fits, reference curves, and guides to the eye.
3. **Reading key:** symbols, colors, line styles, abbreviations, needed axis or unit explanations, and the source of insets or enlarged regions.
4. **Conditions:** relevant samples, geometry, temperature, field, excitation, parameters, or other conditions.
5. **Processing and uncertainty:** consequential normalization, offsets, background subtraction, smoothing, broadening, averaging, and definitions of error bars and shaded bands.
6. **Provenance:** credits for reproduced or adapted material, and method or data sources where required.

Write connected prose. Keep full procedures in Methods and retain what is needed to interpret the display. Match labels and encodings to the actual figure. Use author-supplied numbers and definitions; mark missing essentials in drafts.

For measurements, identify the signal and coordinate conventions, including reference zeros and signs; distinguish direct data from processed or reconstructed displays. For calculations, give the model, consequential parameters, system size, boundary conditions, and averaging as relevant. Explain how uncertainty was obtained: fit uncertainty, variation across samples, and instrument resolution describe different things. For counts, percentages, or repeats, define denominators and independent units; distinguish observed frequencies from model-based estimates.

## Use natural, concrete language

Prefer familiar words and precise verbs; keep subjects near verbs. Replace noun-heavy constructions with verbs when meaning is preserved. Use technical terms for needed precision and explain them for the audience.

Replace generic praise such as “remarkable,” “highly significant,” or “a novel paradigm” with the supporting result, magnitude, comparison, or capability. Verify and support priority claims with the literature; give “significant” a clear statistical or physical meaning.

Remove throat-clearing, repeated summaries, inflated transitions, decorative metaphors, forced lists of three, and headings that fragment short arguments. Use ordinary punctuation and varied sentences. Judge stock phrases by meaning; keep accurate technical uses of “robust,” “interplay,” and “breakdown.”

Preserve the author's language and register. In Chinese, favor direct subjects and verbs over stacked prepositional phrases and empty “perform/do” constructions; in English, choose familiar expressions when equally precise.

Use the same style in replies and progress updates: lead with the answer, finding, or action, supply needed context, and match structure to the task. A short answer can remain one paragraph.

## Revise faithfully

Track what the supplied material supports. Verify added citations and external factual claims; identify specific gaps when sources are unavailable. Keep author queries outside manuscript prose.

Preserve claim strength unless evidence justifies a change. Check edits involving causality, universality, novelty, statistical significance, and mechanism; retain their scientific distinctions.

Consult [Worked examples](references/examples.md) for theory, computation, and experiment in condensed matter, statistical physics, and high-energy/gravity research, and [Sources and editorial decisions](references/sources.md) for the literature and differing conventions.

## Final pass and delivery

Check silently that the question and answer are clear, inferences have evidence or assumptions, and numbers, notation, terms, citations, and figure references agree. Confirm that qualifications carry information, captions match figures and explain consequential details, the opening reaches the subject, and the ending stops at its conclusion.

Return the requested text in usable form. Add brief notes only for material scientific changes, unresolved facts, or requested explanations.
