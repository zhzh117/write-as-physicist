# Sources and editorial decisions

Consult this file when explaining a writing recommendation or choosing between conflicting conventions. The sources below supply complementary guidance on reasoning, prose, equations, captions, and qualification.

## Scientific argument and prose

- **Sayer, E. J. (2019).** [The essentials of effective scientific writing – A revised alternative guide for authors](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.13391). *Functional Ecology*, 33, 1576–1579. Reader-centered structure, paragraph purpose, precise expression, and distinctions between evidence and speculation.
- **Gopen, G. D., and Swan, J. A. (1990).** [The Science of Scientific Writing](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~pel/misc/gopen_swan.pdf). *American Scientist*, 78, 550–558. Information order, sentence structure, and the work a reader must do to connect ideas.
- **Whitesides, G. M. (2004).** [Whitesides' Group: Writing a Paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.200400767). *Advanced Materials*, 16, 1375–1377. Develop the argument from results, figures, and an evolving outline.
- **Mensh, B., and Kording, K. (2017).** [Ten simple rules for structuring papers](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619). *PLOS Computational Biology*, 13, e1005619. Make the central contribution and the logic of each section visible.
- **Hotaling, S. (2020).** [Simple rules for concise scientific writing](https://doi.org/10.1002/lol2.10165). *Limnology and Oceanography Letters*, 5, 379–383. Cut uninformative introductions, repetition, and excess wording while maintaining clarity.
- **Tack, A. J. M., Ehrlén, J., and Roslin, T. (2024).** [How to shorten scientific manuscripts](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.11543). *Ecology and Evolution*, 14, e11543. Evaluate what each clarification or caveat changes and reduce the reader's effort while preserving the argument.
- **Carandini, M. (2022).** [Some Tips for Writing Science](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0497-22.2022). *eNeuro*, 9. Organize around a question, use informative paragraph openings, and keep terminology consistent.
- **Kallestinova, E. D. (2011).** [How to Write Your First Research Paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3178846/). *Yale Journal of Biology and Medicine*, 84, 181–190. Separate structural revision from sentence editing; explain findings, alternatives, and limitations in a coherent discussion.
- **Pautasso, M. (2013).** [Ten Simple Rules for Writing a Literature Review](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003149). *PLOS Computational Biology*, 9, e1003149. Synthesize a literature around questions and evaluate one's own contribution fairly.

## Physics and mathematical exposition

- **Mermin, N. D. (1989).** [What's wrong with these equations?](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2811173). *Physics Today*, 42(10), 9–11. Treat equations as parts of an intelligible written argument.
- **McIntyre, M. E. (1997).** [Lucidity and science I: Writing skills and the pattern perception hypothesis](https://doi.org/10.1179/isr.1997.22.3.199). *Interdisciplinary Science Reviews*, 22, 199–216. Give readers context, use stable terminology, and make relationships explicit.
- **Ashby, M. F. (2011).** [How to Write a Paper](https://utkstair.org/clausius/docs/mse489/pdf/ashby_howtowriteapaper_7ed.pdf), seventh edition. Cambridge engineering writing guide. Planning, concrete language, economical expression, and explicit limitations.
- **Mack, C. A. (2018).** [How to Write a Good Scientific Paper](https://doi.org/10.1117/3.2317707). SPIE. Covers structure, abstracts, figures, revision, and the relation between claims and evidence. [Author's writing resources](https://lithoguru.com/scientist/science-writing.html) provide an open copy and related articles.
- **Chamba, N., Knapen, J. H., and Black, D. (2022).** [How to plan your astronomy research paper in ten steps](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-022-01757-1). *Nature Astronomy*, 6, 1015–1020. Plan a paper around its audience, question, results, and figures. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.12959v2).
- **Knapen, J. H., Chamba, N., and Black, D. (2022).** [How to write and develop your astronomy research paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-022-01759-z). *Nature Astronomy*, 6, 1021–1026. Explain consequential methodological limitations and end with an informative conclusion. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.05503v5).

## Figures and captions

- **Rougier, N. P., Droettboom, M., and Bourne, P. E. (2014).** [Ten Simple Rules for Better Figures](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003833). *PLOS Computational Biology*, 10, e1003833. Rule 4 explains what a caption contributes to reading a figure.
- **Jambor, H., et al. (2021).** [Creating clear and informative image-based figures for scientific publications](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001161). *PLOS Biology*, 19, e3001161. Identify what is shown, explain annotations and insets, and make figures interpretable together with their legends.
- **Cabanski, C., Gilbert, H., and Mosesova, S. (2018).** [Can Graphics Tell Lies? A Tutorial on How To Visualize Your Data](https://doi.org/10.1111/cts.12554). *Clinical and Translational Science*, 11, 371–377. Explains how captions, display choices, and annotations affect interpretation.
- **Caltech Hixon Writing Center.** [Composing Effective Figure Captions in Scientific Articles and Posters](https://writing.caltech.edu/documents/27629/HWC-FigureCaptionHandout.1-2024.pdf). Six-page handout with annotated examples and a useful distinction between article and poster captions.
- **IOP Publishing.** [Example figures](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/questions/example-figures/). Physics examples connect captions to panels, quantities, symbols, and experimental conditions.
- **Annesley, T. M. (2010).** [Bars and Pies Make Better Desserts than Figures](https://doi.org/10.1373/clinchem.2010.152298), *Clinical Chemistry*, 56, 1394–1400; and [Bring Your Best to the Table](https://doi.org/10.1373/clinchem.2010.153502), 56, 1528–1534. Match displays to the comparison, state denominators, and define counts and estimated proportions separately.

## Qualification and conclusions

- **Hyland, K. (1996).** [Writing without conviction? Hedging in science research articles](https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/17.4.433). *Applied Linguistics*, 17, 433–454. Analyzes overlapping accuracy, writer, and reader functions of hedging. A phrase's form alone does not determine its function. [Author-posted text](https://www.academia.edu/7567241/Writing_without_conviction_Hedging_in_scientific_research_articles).
- **Kandel, M. (2016).** [Overhedging](https://style.mla.org/overhedging/). MLA Style Center. Repeated qualification can become redundant or mannered.
- **Kojima, T., and Popiel, H. A. (2023).** [Effective Use of Hedging in Scientific Manuscripts: Advice to Non-Native English-Speaking Researchers](https://doi.org/10.3346/jkms.2023.38.e152). *Journal of Korean Medical Science*, 38, e152. Discusses the relation between qualification and scientific certainty.
- **Kojima, T., and Popiel, H. A. (2024).** [Strategies on Reducing Wordiness to Enhance Readability in Academic Writing](https://doi.org/10.3346/jkms.2024.39.e240). *Journal of Korean Medical Science*, 39, e240. Distinguishes excess wording from meaningful qualification.
- **Sayer, E. J., and Ferry, L. A. (2023).** [Demonstrating a significant advance to the field](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.14458). *Functional Ecology*, 37, 2980–2983. Encourages conclusions that explain the knowledge gained and the specific questions it creates.
- **Lingard, L. (2026).** [Rhetorical Candour: When and How to Say It Straight in Your Academic Manuscript](https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.2269). *Perspectives on Medical Education*, 15, 251–256. Relates the directness of a claim to the evidence and reasoning supporting it.
- **Jain, N., and Pilmane, M. (2026).** [What makes a study limitation? An inference-first approach to reporting uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1093/rescon/vmag186). *Research Connections*, 1(4), vmag186. Connects each limitation to the inference it constrains and the consequence for interpretation.

## Decisions where guidance differs

**Qualifications.** The operational definition in the main file concerns qualifications that add no useful information. Scientific uncertainty can involve several distinct issues in one sentence. Identify each issue before shortening it. Preserve the evidence relationship when replacing a verb; changing “suggests” to “demonstrates” changes the claim.

**Endings.** Use the final sentence for the paper's actual answer. A null result, a failed model, or the inability to distinguish mechanisms can be that answer. Repeated disclaimers after an already scoped finding add little. A specific unresolved question can be valuable when it follows from the result.

**Caption openings.** Guides differ on whether to open with the subject or with a finding, and on how much interpretation belongs in the caption. Follow the target venue and figure's function. In either form, make the panel descriptions and scientific conditions accurate.

**Style preferences.** Authors differ on first person, passive voice, title form, paragraph length, and wording. Use these suggestions as editing options. Adopt the choice that makes the physical meaning clearest for the intended reader.

**Source quality.** A writing guide can contain weak advice. For example, Lippi's 2017 [personal perspective](https://doi.org/10.21037/atm.2017.07.43) suggests leaving some limitations for reviewers to identify. The present skill requires consequential limitations to be stated where they affect interpretation. Other guides' fixed word counts, categorical word bans, and statistical shortcuts are evaluated in the same way.
